# HIS Simulator — Student Guide

**Course:** PHD 111 · ICT for Pharmacy
**Topic:** Pharmacy and the Development of IT
**Lab time:** 2 hours

## Before you start

- Use a laptop if possible. Phones work but are cramped.
- Use one browser, and do not use a private or incognito window. Your progress is saved in that browser.
- Hospital Bertam, its patients and staff are fictional.

## Getting started

1. Open the link your lecturer gives you.
2. Enter your name, matric number, group and **track**:
   - **Pharmacy assistant (Diploma)** or **Pharmacist (Degree)**, as your lecturer instructs.
3. Answer the **pre-test** (10 questions). It is fine not to know the answers yet.
4. The **Cases** panel opens. Start with Case 1.

## Controls (top bar)

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **BHIS / IHIS / THIS** | Changes the hospital's HIS tier. Modules appear or disappear. |
| **Logged in as** | Switches your role: clerk, doctor, pharmacist, pharmacy assistant, lab officer, nurse, finance officer, IT/admin. |
| **Start downtime drill** | Takes the system offline to practise paper procedures. |
| **Cases** | Your case list: steps, hints, auto-ticks and reflection boxes. |
| **Glossary** | Search any term: eGFR, PRN, indent, PACS… |
| **Post-test** | Take it at the end. You can only submit once. |
| **Download report** | Saves your results, test scores, reflections and activity log. |

Each module only opens for the right role. Opening one with the wrong role is denied and logged, as in a real hospital.

## Tips

- **Read every screening check.** Some pass, some need action, and some only need counselling.
- **Pharmacy assistants refer; pharmacists decide.** If you are the assistant and see a major alert, use **Refer to pharmacist**.
- **Enter paper prescriptions exactly as written.** If you make a mistake, **Cancel entry** and enter it again.
- **Intervene** creates a new order that must be screened again.
- **Verify** and **Dispense** are separate steps. Dispensing needs the Konsep 5B checklist.
- **Never issue expired stock.** Record a reason whenever you supply less than requested.
- Use the **Hint** in each case if you are stuck.

## Reflection questions

Answer each one in 3–5 sentences in the Cases panel. Link your answers to the lecture.

1. Which other modules can now see Encik Rahman's data, and why does a single MRN matter?
2. What could have happened with a handwritten prescription and no electronic allergy screening?
3. In a BHIS hospital, lab results stay on paper. How would the pharmacist catch the metformin problem there?
4. Why do some alerts need action while others only need counselling? What is "alert fatigue"?
5. Which of the five rights does the system check for you, and which still depend on people?
6. How does a computerised inventory reduce pilferage and expired stock compared with manual stock cards?
7. What risks arise during downtime, and why does back-entry matter for the patient's record?
8. Your hospital is moving from IHIS to THIS. Which two changes would affect pharmacists most?
9. Why must every access to a patient record be logged?
10. Why must a transcriber enter a prescription exactly as written, even when they notice a problem?
11. How does recording partial supplies in the system help the ward, the pharmacy and the store?
12. Which label error is most dangerous, and how do printed labels reduce errors?
